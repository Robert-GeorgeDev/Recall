At Octom we want the service to be usable by as many people as possible, however they interact with their device or the interface.

We try to build the interface so that it can be used by people who navigate with a keyboard, use a screen reader, enlarge text, or access the service on screens of different sizes.

Accessibility is an ongoing process, and this page describes our current approach.

## 1. What we do

When building Octom we follow accessibility principles such as:

### Semantic structure

Where possible we use semantic HTML elements and a logical page structure.

Headings, sections, forms, buttons and interactive controls are designed so that people and assistive technologies can identify and use them more easily.

### Keyboard navigation

We aim for the main features to be usable without a mouse.

Interactive elements must be able to receive focus and follow a logical navigation order.

### Visible focus indicators

Interactive elements must have visible focus states so that keyboard users can tell which element is active.

### Forms and labels

We try to provide clear labels and instructions for form fields and to associate error messages correctly with the relevant fields.

### Contrast and legibility

We choose colors and visual combinations with text legibility and the distinction of important elements in mind.

Where possible we do not rely on color alone to convey essential information.

### Reflow and mobile devices

The interface is designed to work at different screen sizes and to allow use on mobile devices.

We aim for the main information and features to remain accessible when content is enlarged or the screen is small.

### Motion and animation

Where we use animation or motion effects, we try to respect the system preference for reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`).

When the device or browser indicates that the user prefers reduced motion, some animations and transitions may be reduced or removed.

### Messages and interface states

We try to make important application states visible and easy to understand, such as:

* loading;
* successful operations;
* errors;
* form validation;
* confirmations;
* actions that change or delete data.

## 2. Standards we follow

In designing and building Octom we follow the principles of the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)**.

Our goal is to approach **WCAG 2.1 AA** for the relevant parts of the service.

WCAG gives recommendations for web content accessibility and covers aspects such as:

* whether information can be perceived;
* whether the interface can be operated;
* whether content and interactions can be understood;
* compatibility with assistive technologies.

Following these principles does not mean that every Octom component currently meets every WCAG criterion.

## 3. Where we are

Octom's accessibility is being improved continuously.

At the moment we have **not carried out a complete independent accessibility audit** and we do not claim that Octom fully conforms to WCAG 2.1 AA.

There may be components, pages or situations where the experience is not yet optimal for some people or assistive technologies.

Accessibility can also be affected by:

* the browser;
* the operating system;
* the device;
* the assistive technology used;
* the user's configuration;
* services or components provided by third parties.

When we find accessibility problems, we try to assess and fix them according to severity and impact.

## 4. Current limitations

Although we work to improve accessibility, we cannot guarantee that every component of the service is accessible to every user in every combination of device, browser and assistive technology.

New features or features under development may have accessibility problems that have not yet been identified.

Third-party services and content may also have their own accessibility limitations that are not entirely under our control.

## 5. Tell us about a problem

User feedback helps us find problems that internal testing may miss.

If you run into an accessibility problem, please contact us at:

**[contact@octom.eu](mailto:contact@octom.eu)**

You can also use the contact form on the website.

If possible, include:

* the page or feature where the problem occurs;
* a description of the problem;
* the device you use;
* your browser and its version;
* the assistive technology you use, if any;
* the steps needed to reproduce the problem;
* any other information that could help us understand the situation.

You do not need to provide medical or other sensitive information to report an accessibility problem.

We will review the feedback and try to fix the problems found within a reasonable time, depending on severity, impact and technical complexity.

If a feature creates difficulties for you, you can also contact us to ask for an alternative way to access that information or function, where possible.
